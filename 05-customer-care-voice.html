7 Customer Care Principles

1

Bring quality into the conversation

Customers should feel the same care in our support that they expect from the product. Listen properly, give accurate guidance, follow through.

2

Understand what the customer is trying to achieve

A product issue is rarely only technical - it may interrupt a routine, a coffee ritual, a meal, a gift, or an expectation the customer had when they chose the product.

3

Use process with judgement

Processes support good service, they don't replace good judgement. When a situation doesn't fit, ask for guidance and escalate appropriately.

4

Be knowledgeable and honest

When we don't know, we don't guess. We say we'll check, escalate, or confirm - then follow through.

5

Make the next step clear

Every conversation should give clarity: what's needed, what happens next, what to expect.

6

Own the experience within your role

Do what we said we'd do, keep the customer informed, don't leave a dead end. If a case has dragged on, consider escalation or goodwill.

7

Match the customer

First-time users need simple reassurance; coffee enthusiasts want precise technical guidance; frustrated customers need calm, clear ownership before troubleshooting.

Framework for a Positive Customer Experience

Our voice exists to make the customer feel three things:

HeardWe listened and understood why it matters
GuidedThey know what we need, why, and what's next
ConfidentHandled by someone capable, honest, in control

Four Pillars: show empathy · use practical compassion · stay professional · do not take it personally.

The ANCHOR Model

ANCHOR is not a script - it's an acronym for the 6 behaviours we move through on every call, in order. Each letter is one beat in the conversation: it starts with understanding the customer, moves through solving the problem, and ends with the customer feeling looked after. It's the daily reminder of how we want customers to feel - simple enough to remember, practical enough to use live on a call.

What Each Letter Stands For

Read the flow left to right. Each tile below expands into its full behaviour and example line in the table further down.

A Acknowledge Listen first. Recognize the impact on the customer, not just the technical fault.
→
N Navigate Don't stop at the first obstacle. Check and find the right path before promising an outcome.
→
C Commit Commit to a concrete next step: what happens, who does it, and by when.
→
H Honour Honour the customer's time, trust, and investment in choosing the brand.
→
O Own Own the issue until it's resolved, or hand it over with full context so the customer never repeats themselves.
→
R Reassure Reassure through clarity and confidence, not by promising a specific outcome.
Say it in one breath Acknowledge the problem → Navigate to the real cause → Commit to a next step → Honour their trust → Own it until it's done → Reassure them it's handled. That order is the whole model.

Full Detail: Behaviour and Example Line per Step

StepBehaviourExample line
A - AcknowledgeListen properly before responding; recognize impact, not just the technical detail"I can understand why this is disappointing, especially when you chose the product expecting it to last."
N - NavigateDon't stop at the first obstacle; check, escalate, find the right path without promising outcomes too early"Let me look at the full situation first, including the age of the product and what options may be available."
C - CommitCommit clearly to the next step - what happens, who does it, when"I will review this with our escalation team and come back to you by tomorrow with the next step."
H - HonourHonour the customer's time, trust, and investment in the brand"I appreciate that when you invest in a premium appliance, you expect it to last and to be supported properly."
O - OwnOwn the experience until resolved or properly handed over, with full context passed on"I am going to move this to the team that can approve the next option, but I will make sure they have the full background."
R - ReassureReassure through clarity, confidence, capable support - not a promised outcome"I will make sure we review this properly and give you a clear answer based on the full situation."
Objective Sound like knowledgeable humans who listen properly, take ownership, and guide the customer to the best next step. Resolve on first contact wherever possible; where that's not possible, make the next step clear and accountable.
Merge note Acknowledge and Honour are both empathetic, pre-diagnosis moves - recognizing the problem's impact and recognizing the customer's trust/investment - and can be delivered as one combined opening beat on a live call: A+H → N → C → O → R. Call Flow Scripts

Start Well

"Thank you for calling Sage Appliances Support, this is [name]. How can I help today?"
"Have you contacted us about this before, or is this the first time?"
"Can you talk me through what has happened?"

Set the Path

"To help properly, I'll need to check a few details. I'll explain why I'm asking as we go."
"Once I have the details, I'll explain the best next step and any options available."

Understand the Goal

Ask open questions. Review the full situation.

Close with Clarity

Explain the next step. Close with confidence.

Situation Examples

SituationWhat to say
Premium product issue"I can understand why this is disappointing, especially when you chose a premium product expecting it to perform well. Let me look at the full situation and explain the best next step."
Product not working"Let's start with a quick check so we can understand what is happening. I'll guide you through it step by step."

Handling Angry Customers

Key principles: stay calm and set your tone · acknowledge emotions without amplifying them · do not apologise excessively · redirect to what you can do · never match their energy or become defensive · set professional limits if needed.

SituationResponse
Raised voice or venting"I can understand why you're frustrated. I'm here to help you through this and focus on the next step."
Feels ignored or passed around"I'll take ownership of the next step from here. I'll review the full background, so you do not have to start again."
Demands a manager"Of course, I can help with that. Before I arrange the next step, let me capture the full picture so the right person can review it properly."
Threatens negative review"I understand you want this taken seriously. The best thing I can do now is review the full situation and focus on the right next step."
Abusive language"I want to help, but I'll need us to keep the conversation respectful so I can do that effectively."
Angry about policy"Let me look at the full situation and explain what options are available within our guidelines."

What Not to Say / What to Say Instead

AvoidUse instead
"There is nothing I can do.""Let me look at the full situation and explain what options may be available."
"As you have been advised.""As we discussed earlier, the next step is..."
"I don't know.""That's not something I know off the top of my head, but let me find out for you."

"You'll have to wait.""I appreciate your patience. Here is what will happen next, and when you can expect an update."

General Tips & Formula

Listen actively · use empathy with purpose (acknowledge without over-apologising or amplifying) · be proactive (lead on the next step, don't leave the customer wondering) · use clear language (what we can do / need to check / customer can expect) · know when to escalate (ask for guidance rather than a flat answer too early).

Our formula Empathy + Expertise + Ownership - "We understand why this matters. We'll review it properly and guide you through the best next step."

Activity score card rates each call Low / Medium / High on: Acknowledge · Navigate · Commit · Honour · Own · Reassure.

Worked Example

Issue: "My Barista Express keeps shutting off mid-brew. The lights flicker, and the machine powers down before it finishes pulling a shot."

Acknowledge"I understand how upsetting it is when your Barista Express shuts off mid-brew, especially when you're counting on it for a smooth coffee routine."
Navigate"Let's walk through a few quick checks together to see whether it's a power connection issue, an internal sensor fault, or something else interrupting the brew cycle."
Commit"I'll stay with you through each step until we pinpoint exactly what's causing the shutdown."
Honour"You deserve a machine that performs consistently, and we'll treat this issue with the seriousness it deserves."
Own"I'll take full responsibility for guiding you through the troubleshooting and arranging the right solution if needed."
Reassure"We'll get this sorted, whether it's a simple reset or a repair, you won't be left without support."

What We Should Avoid

Treating the guarantee as the only measure of fairness · saying no too early without reviewing the full situation · implying goodwill is automatic · promising a repair/replacement/refund/upgrade/exception before approval · making the customer repeat their story unnecessarily · hiding behind internal process · sending the customer between teams without a clear handover · using technical language that doesn't help the customer · sounding surprised the customer expected the product to last · closing a case because the process is complete when the customer experience is still unresolved.

Customer Experience Standard

A good interaction should leave the customer thinking: "They listened." · "They understood why this mattered." · "They were honest with me." · "They looked at the full situation." · "They knew what they were talking about." · "I know what happens next." · "I still feel I chose the right brand."
